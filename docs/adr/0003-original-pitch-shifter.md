# ADR-0003: Original pitch shifter (sweeping delay line) instead of an LGPL/GPL library

**Status:** accepted · **Date:** 2026-09-03 · **Revised:** 2026-09-04 (algorithm), 2026-09-20 (two taps; corrected account of the first attempt)

## Context
"Preserve tempo" retune needs a pitch shifter in an AudioWorklet. Mature options are SoundTouch (LGPL 2.1, WASM builds) and Rubber Band (GPL / commercial). The suite is MIT-licensed and ships bundled JavaScript; LGPL compliance for a bundled WASM/JS library is legally awkward and GPL is incompatible.

## Decision
Implement an original, MIT-licensed time-domain pitch shifter (`packages/audio-core/src/worklets`). It preserves duration, is CPU-light and deterministic, and its added latency is measured and exposed to group mode for compensation.

The first implementation used overlap-add granular shifting: two permanently overlapping Hann grains, each sweeping its own delay. Offline measurement (`renderThroughCore` + `estimateFundamentalHz`) showed it did not deliver the requested shift — at the 432 Hz reference (ratio 0.9818) a 440 Hz tone came out at 440.0 Hz, no shift at all.

The explanation recorded here at the time was that overlap-add *as a method* cancels the shift: that two grains drifting `(1 − ratio) · hop` apart sum to a resultant whose phase sweep contributes `f · (1 − ratio)` and cancels the intended `f · ratio`. **That was wrong, and it is corrected here.** Overlap-add pitch shifting is a standard, working technique; what failed was that implementation. Its grains advanced on a fixed hop that was not locked to the delay sweep, so each grain restarted at a delay unrelated to where the previous one had swept to, and the intended shift was undone at every hop. The measurement was real; the conclusion drawn from it was about the code, not about the family of algorithms.

The shipped algorithm is a **two-tap sweeping delay line**. A read tap's delay changes by `(1 − ratio)` per sample, so inside a sweep the pitch is exactly `ratio`. A second tap is held exactly half a window from the first, and each is weighted by a raised cosine over the full window, `g(d) = ½ − ½·cos(2πd/W)`. Because the taps are half a window apart their window arguments differ by π, so the two gains sum to unity at every sample — and, decisively, each gain is exactly zero at the delay where its own tap wraps. The wrap is therefore multiplied by nothing instead of being crossfaded across.

The version between these two — one tap, re-centred at the end of the window and equal-power crossfaded from its old position — shipped with a defect. During that crossfade the outgoing tap's delay kept being stepped by `(1 − ratio)`, so it walked past the end of the window and went negative: the tap read *ahead* of the write pointer, which on a circular buffer means a whole buffer of stale audio, spliced in at full amplitude. Measured as the largest step between consecutive output samples on a 0.5-amplitude 440 Hz sine at 48 kHz, that was 2.2× the input's own largest step at a semitone and 8.8× at ratio 1.25. Under the two-tap design the same measurement is exactly `ratio×` — the theoretical floor, since a tap reading `ratio` times faster steepens the waveform by `ratio` and nothing else is added. `packages/audio-core/scripts/measure-clicks.ts` prints the table and `tests/unit/pitch-shifter.test.ts` gates it at 2×.

## Consequences
- Accuracy where it matters. Measured on a 440 Hz tone at 48 kHz: 432 Hz reference 0.09 % error, one semitone up 0.40 %, a fifth up (ratio 1.5) 2.2 %. Small retunes are effectively exact; large shifts wrap often and sound rougher, and the UI says so.
- Two taps read different moments of the recording at once, so they comb against each other: sustained tones warble gently and transients can be doubled, most audibly at large ratios. This is continuous rather than concentrated at a wrap, which is why it reads as warble rather than as clicks. Each tap crosses the window every `W / (|1 − ratio| · sampleRate)` seconds — about 0.72 s at a semitone, 0.17 s at ratio 1.25.
- Because the gains sum to unity rather than to √2, the shifter never exceeds the input's peak. The −1 dBFS limiter after the EQ stays, but it is no longer compensating for the shifter.
- Mean added latency stays near half the sweep window (1024 samples ≈ 21 ms at 48 kHz), swinging by roughly ±5 % of the window as the taps sweep; either tap's instantaneous delay ranges across the whole window. The mean is reported through `getLatency()` and subtracted from group-mode position reports.
- "Linked speed" remains available as the honest alternative where pitch and tempo change together.
- The regressions are locked down by unit tests that measure rendered output rather than asserting on internals: the shift itself, the absence of discontinuities, unity peak, and finiteness over ten seconds. A future algorithm change cannot silently stop shifting, nor silently start clicking.
- Loading the worklet is itself gated. The DSP being correct is not the same as the browser being able to run it: `music-player/tests/e2e/worklet.spec.ts` loads the built asset into a real `AudioContext`, after a period in which the served build shipped the worklet as uncompiled TypeScript and retune was dead in the deployed app while every unit test passed.
